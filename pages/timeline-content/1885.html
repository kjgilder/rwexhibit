Administrative Crackdown and Student Resistance

When RWU President William Henry Stifler attempted to suppress the dissent with threats of expulsion, over 100 students requested honorable dismissal, effectively forcing the school to shut down by February 1887.

Institutional Response and Leadership Change

Although the American Baptist Home Mission Society (ABHMS) initially dismissed the claims, advocacy from local trustees, several of whom resigned in protest, eventually led to Stifler’s replacement by William Edwin Morang. Historians consider this pivotal moment emblematic of the strained relationship between Black southern Baptists and their northern benefactors, demonstrating that students were active agents in demanding racial equity and self-determination within their own institutions.
