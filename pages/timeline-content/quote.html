Video Archive: Education the Key to Freedom

Source: PBS - The Citizenship Project

After the Civil War, newly freed African Americans across the South placed enormous value on education, seeing it as essential to independence and self-determination. Many who escaped slavery gathered in Union-controlled cities like Nashville, where missionaries, Black churches, and the Freedmen’s Bureau established schools for formerly enslaved children and adults. These early classrooms laid the foundation for a network of Black educational institutions across Tennessee. Among them was Roger Williams College in Nashville, founded by Black Baptists to provide higher education and leadership training for African Americans during Reconstruction. Institutions like Roger Williams helped transform the belief that education meant freedom into lasting opportunities for Black communities, preparing generations of teachers, ministers, and civic leaders who would continue the struggle for equality.
