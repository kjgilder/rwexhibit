Educational Impact and Notable Alumni

After 1908, Roger Williams University (RWU) entered its final chapter, characterized by a renewed sense of Black autonomy following its relocation and reorganization. The school continued to produce influential graduates, such as Rebecca Jane Perkins Hill (Betty Hill), whose civil rights work in Los Angeles reflected the university’s enduring mission of leadership and social change.

Role Within Nashville’s Black Educational Network

RWU remained part of a dynamic network of Black institutions in Nashville, alongside Fisk University, Meharry Medical College, and the Tennessee Agricultural & Industrial State Normal School (now Tennessee State University), contributing to a broader culture of Black higher education.

Mounting Institutional Challenges

By the 1920s, the university faced severe financial strain, denominational divisions, increased competition from better-funded institutions, and declining external support, all of which weakened its long-term stability. In December 1929, Roger Williams University’s independent era came to an end through its merger with the Howe Institute in Memphis, with its legacy continuing through what is now LeMoyne-Owen College.
