Early Life and Foundations

Rebecca Jane Perkins Hill, often known as Betty Hill, was born in Nashville, Tennessee, and attended Davidson County’s first Black school during her childhood. In 1898, she married Army Sergeant Abraham Houston Hill, and together they later helped establish the Los Angeles chapter of the NAACP.

Leadership and Activism

During the 1920s, Hill organized the Westside Property Owners Association to protect the rights of African American homeowners and raised funds to support educational scholarships. In 1926, she initiated a lawsuit challenging segregation in Los Angeles public swimming pools, which resulted in a landmark 1931 court victory that ended pool segregation in the city. While awaiting the court’s decision, she also founded the Republican Women’s Study Club and, in 1940, became the first African American woman to serve as a delegate in the organization.

Rebecca Jane Perkins "Betty" Hill pictured with a group of community leaders, illustrating her deep involvement in civic circles.

Legacy and Impact

That same year, she successfully advocated for the construction of a larger community swimming pool, which opened with a plaque honoring her leadership. Hill passed away on May 12, 1960, leaving behind a lasting legacy of civic engagement and community advocacy in Los Angeles.
